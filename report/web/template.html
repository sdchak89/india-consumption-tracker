The short answer

  1. Merchant UPI is still growing about 25% a year, and the slowdown has levelled off.₹101.5 lakh crore in Oct 2025–Sep 2026, up 26.4%, from ₹23.0 lakh crore four years earlier. Monthly growth has stayed between 22% and 31% since early 2025.
  2. Merchant UPI's lead over consumption growth is narrowing.Merchant UPI grew 26.5% in FY26 against 8.5% for consumption in rupees. The gap (UPI growing relative to consumption) has shrunk from 50% (FY24) to 29% to 17%, and the average merchant payment has stopped shrinking (₹595 → ₹601 per payment, comparing the latest two October–September years). Slower UPI growth ahead need not mean weaker demand.
  3. Cash's spending share fell, while cash balances kept rising.Cash's model-estimated share of consumer spending fell from 70–78% (Apr–Jun 2022) to 39–48% (Apr–Jun 2026). ATM withdrawals fell ₹1.9 lakh crore in FY26, yet cash held by the public rose 12%.
  4. Food is the engine; liquor and paan shops are among the fastest growers.Groceries are the largest named category (₹9.5 lakh crore). Fast food grew 108% a year for four years. Liquor-shop UPI is up 70% with an unchanged average payment.
  5. Vehicle buying and loan balances rebounded together.Car and two-wheeler registrations grew 21–22% in the year after the September 2025 GST cut (5–6% the year before). NBFC consumer-durable loans grew 56%; bank vehicle loans 6.1% → 15.5%. Credit data add financing context; UPI cannot identify how a payment was funded.
  6. Real consumption is growing; prices and confidence are the watch-points.Real consumption grew 7.2% in FY26. Recent company sales reflect price and mix as well as volume (HUL: sales +15%, volume +7%); RBI urban confidence fell from 96.9 to 87.9, inflation is 5.5% and the RBI raised rates in October.
0 · Scope

What part of UPI does this study cover?

By value, merchant payments are only 30% of UPI. But they are 63% of all UPI payments, and they are where consumption shows up. Named consumer categories cover 11% of all UPI value, and NPCI puts another 45% of merchant value into an unnamed "Others" bucket.

UPI in the last 12 months (Oct 2025–Sep 2026)

P2P = person to person (transfers to family, rent, small sellers using personal UPI IDs). P2M = person to merchant (payments to a registered merchant code). "Consumption" = named categories of household spending (food, fuel, bills, shopping, health, alcohol); it includes some mixed categories such as uniforms, marketplaces and department stores. "Financial" = brokers, loan repayments and digital gold.

Source: NPCI UPI P2P/P2M table and merchant category table. Data: data/npci/p2p_p2m_monthly.csv, data/npci/merchant_categories_monthly.csv

Data

Why P2P is left out. P2P averages ₹2,438 a payment against ₹601 for merchants; most of it is money moving between people, not spending. Some consumption does hide in P2P (a vegetable seller paid on a personal UPI ID), but NPCI does not split it out, so it cannot be measured. That means merchant UPI understates UPI's role in consumption.

1 · Direction

Is spending through UPI still growing?

Yes, by about a quarter a year. The steep slowdown of 2022–24 is consistent with UPI's adoption surge fading (adoption is not measured directly); since early 2025 monthly value growth has held between 22% and 31%.

2 · Consumption signal

Is UPI growth a measure of consumption?

Partly, and more so every year. In FY26 merchant UPI grew 26.5% while consumption grew 8.5% in rupees. In an accounting split, about 35% of UPI's growth matches consumption growth (19% in FY24); the rest is merchant UPI growing relative to consumption, which includes switching from cash and cards and payments that are not consumption. The split does not say where each rupee came from.

Data

Cash held and cash withdrawn are moving apart. Cash held is not shrinking: currency with the public rose from ₹36.3 to ₹40.7 lakh crore in FY26 (+12.0%), faster than consumption. Cash withdrawn is: ATM withdrawals fell from ₹30.6 to ₹28.7 lakh crore. Our inference People may be keeping cash as a store of value while spending less of it; the data show the two trends, not the transfer. UPI's extra ₹19.1 lakh crore sits alongside consumption growth of ₹15.4 lakh crore across all channels. The two cover different payments, so the gap between them is not a measure of switching.

Where this goes. The share-gain part fell from 50% (FY24) to 29% to 17% (FY26), down by about 42% each year. Scenario If nominal consumption keeps growing 8.5% a year and the share-gain part halves from here, merchant UPI growth would be about 17.5% next year and 13% the year after, tracking consumption more closely.

Consistent with maturing adoption: the average merchant payment has stopped shrinking. It fell from ₹813 (Oct 2021–Sep 2022) to ₹664, ₹643 and ₹595 (Oct 2024–Sep 2025), then edged up to ₹601 (Oct 2025–Sep 2026). Year on year it has been flat or slightly up every month since January 2026, after falling 6–10% year on year in Jan–Aug 2025 and narrowing to about 1.5–4.1% in Sep–Dec. The fixed 13-category basket shows the same: ₹272.50 → ₹272.66. The sub-1% rise is nominal and mix-sensitive; it does not establish larger real purchases.

3 · Cash vs digital

How much of consumer spending is still cash?

On a model estimate, between 39% and 48% in Apr–Jun 2026, down from 70–78% in Apr–Jun 2022. In rupees, digital consumer payments tripled from ₹8.9 to ₹27.7 lakh crore a quarter, while cash spending fell from ₹25.2 to ₹21.3 lakh crore.

4 · UPI and merchants

How big is UPI next to GDP, and what does it mean for merchants?

All UPI moved ₹314 lakh crore in FY26, 0.91 times GDP (0.53 times in FY23). Merchant UPI reached ₹90.9 lakh crore, equal in size to 46% of consumer spending (₹196.2 lakh crore of PFCE in FY26; 21% in FY23). For merchants, free acceptance ends for large payments at large shops.

Data

The new merchant fee. Accepting UPI has been free since 2020; the government compensates banks through an incentive scheme instead (₹2,000 crore budgeted for FY27, covering RuPay debit and low-value BHIM-UPI payments). The Finance Ministry has notified a fee of 0.4% (capped at ₹300) on payments above ₹2,000 at merchants receiving more than ₹1 lakh a month for three consecutive months; a flat ₹5 above ₹2,000 for fuel, telecom, rail, insurance and utilities; 0.02% for capital markets. Payments of ₹2,000 or less, small merchants, person-to-person transfers and recurring mandates (AutoPay) stay free. It was due on 15 October 2026 and has been deferred to after Diwali; the new date is to be notified.

Our inference The fee touches few payments but a large share of value: brokers, utilities, electronics and clothing sit near or above the threshold. At 0.4% UPI stays far cheaper than cards, so merchants are unlikely to switch. Watch for bills split into ₹2,000 pieces, cash discounts on big purchases, and banks using the new income to fund merchant credit and devices.

5 · Category by category

What is India paying for, and how fast is each part growing?

Food and fuel lead, and everyday payments are getting smaller as UPI reaches the kirana. Money sent to brokers and lenders behaves differently: it follows market rules and reclassifications, not spending. Clothing, electronics, online marketplaces and department stores will be revisited once the October–November festive data are out (expected December 2026); see Limits.

Terms used in this section
Value: rupees paid through UPI to merchants in that category.
Payments: number of UPI transactions (count).
Average payment: value ÷ payments, ₹ per transaction ("ticket size").
Growth: Oct 2025–Sep 2026 vs Oct 2024–Sep 2025, using only months NPCI published in both years.
CAGR: compound annual growth from the earliest Oct–Sep year with at least 9 calendar months published in both that year and the latest year (matched months) to Oct 2025–Sep 2026.
vs all merchant UPI: the same CAGR for all merchant UPI over the same years. Beating it means the category gained share.
MCC: merchant category code, the 4-digit code a bank assigns to each merchant. NPCI groups payments by it.
NPCI "Others": merchant payments outside the top 29 categories NPCI publishes each month (45% of value).
Reclassification break: a one-time drop or jump when payments move to a different MCC. Growth across a break compares unlike things.
Break-adjusted growth: a sensitivity calculation, not measured like-for-like growth: months before a suspected break are rescaled by the size of the one-month drop.

6 · Substitution

Do related categories move together, or does one eat the other?

They mostly move together. There is no evidence of substitution in these UPI shares: eating out is not replacing cooking at home. Within categories, UPI payments have moved towards liquor shops over bars and towards smaller, more frequent payments. Whether people are buying differently or just paying differently (channel migration) is not measured.

7 · Tax cuts

Did the income-tax relief and the GST cut change spending?

Vehicle buying jumped after the GST cut (rate cuts came at the same time, and neither is isolated). The income-tax relief coincides with faster consumption nationally and a shift of UPI toward high-taxpayer states, but not with faster UPI spending overall; neither effect can be isolated.

What we see. Consumer UPI growth slowed from 44.3% (FY25) to 29.6% (FY26) as adoption matured, and discretionary slowed more (45.5% → 26.2%) than essentials (43.9% → 30.6%). Of the six largest consumer categories, only restaurants accelerated (26.2% → 31.4%). Across states, the most-taxed group went from losing UPI share (−8.2) to holding it (+0.4), while the least-taxed group went from gaining (+6.9) to losing (−2.6). Nationally, real consumption growth rose from 6.4% (FY25) to 7.2% (FY26).

Should credit be isolated? Yes, but only in part. RuPay credit cards, the only ones that can be linked to UPI, were about 0.7% of merchant UPI in FY24 (April 2023–March 2024) and 1.6% in April–October 2024, the first seven months of FY25 and the latest published, so most UPI spending is paid from bank balances (newer credit lines on UPI are not published, so this can't be fully confirmed). We also remove loan repayments and broker payments. What cannot be isolated: a personal loan, once in the bank account, can be spent by UPI; the purchases tax relief would fund most (phones, appliances, two-wheelers) are increasingly bought on EMI; and down-payments and EMIs can be paid by UPI. Credit data add financing context, but UPI cannot identify the funding source, and no financing share has been isolated (section 8).

Verdict: ◐ Consistent with a tax effect, not proof The state pattern fits the relief, but neither test isolates it: Maharashtra alone drives most of the top group's earlier fall, and the share of UPI that NPCI cannot place in a state jumped from 26% to 42% in Apr–Jun 2025, which can move state shares on its own.

8 · Credit

What does credit add to the UPI picture?

Credit data add financing context; UPI cannot show how a payment was funded. Consumer credit accelerated over the past year, led by gold loans, vehicle loans and NBFC durable loans. It sits inside a broad credit upswing (all bank credit grew 19.4% to September 2026, up from 11.4%), and lenders don't report what the loans are spent on.

Where the extra ₹11.2 lakh crore went (banks Sep 2025 → Sep 2026, NBFCs Aug → Aug, housing excluded): vehicle loans ₹1.9 lakh crore (17%), consumer durables ₹0.3 lakh crore (3%), education ₹0.2 lakh crore (2%), credit cards ₹0.2 lakh crore (2%), gold loans ₹4.1 lakh crore (37%), personal and other loans ₹4.5 lakh crore (40%). These are shares of the net increase in outstanding balances, not new loans disbursed. Vehicle, durable and education loans are normally taken to buy a specific item (23.5% with cards). Gold, personal and other loans make up the other 76.5%, and their end-use is unobserved: they may fund spending, but the data can't say how much.

9 · The consumer

Is the Indian consumer actually spending more?

Yes, in real terms, by about 7% a year. UPI overstates the pace because it is still taking share, and underneath, recent company sales reflect price as well as volume, and confidence has weakened.

Vehicle buying rebounded after the GST cut✓ Yes; policy role plausible, not isolatedRegistrations 5–6% → 21–22% growth; FADA October 2025 +40.5%. No clear sign yet that it pulled demand forward from later months (Navratri 2026 per-day registrations +2.8%).
Eating out is replacing cooking at home✕ No evidence in UPI sharesEating out's share of food UPI flat at 33–35% since 2023. Domino's same-store +4.1% covers all channels, so it does not measure dine-in; food delivery +18–22%. Channel migration is unmeasured.
Credit is funding vehicle and durable purchases✓ Very likelyThese loans are normally taken to buy the item, with the vehicle as collateral (some lenders also offer top-ups and refinancing, so this can't be certified). Vehicle loans added ₹1.9 lakh crore (banks 6.1% → 15.5% growth; NBFCs ~15%) in the same year registrations jumped 21–22%. NBFC consumer-durable loans +56%. Commercial vehicles (goods carriers +25%) are financed mainly through business loans: bank credit to transport operators grew 24.6% (it also funds working capital).
Credit is funding everyday spending more broadly◐ Possible, not measured76.5% of the ₹11.2 lakh crore added to non-housing consumer credit is in gold loans (37%) and personal and other loans (40%); end-use is unobserved. Lenders don't report what that money is spent on (medical bills, school fees, repaying other debt, a small business or shopping). Higher gold prices let households borrow more against the same jewellery; they don't raise existing balances by themselves. Counterpoint expects 42% of smartphones to be bought on finance in 2026 (35% in 2025).

10 · Geography

Where is UPI activity concentrated, and how fast is each state growing?

The south and west lead on UPI money moved per person; Bihar, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand trail. These are gross payment flows, not income or spending: UPI counts money each time it moves, including transfers between people and between a person's own accounts, so all UPI per person (about ₹20,900 a month, ₹2.5 lakh a year) is close to GDP per person (₹2.4 lakh in FY26). Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Jammu and Kashmir grew fastest; West Bengal grew slowest in value (+11.7%) though its payment count rose 18.5%. Among the eight cities covered, Pune (about ₹65,600 a month) and Bengaluru (₹61,600) show the highest figures on World Population Review's 2026 populations. Those populations are not matched to the districts NPCI reports: mostly they cover wider metro areas, so Hyderabad (0.5× Telangana's average) and Kolkata (about West Bengal's average) come out low, while Pune's is smaller than its district and its figure is overstated. The method note below explains.

How the geography numbers are calculated (all states and cities)

Source and attribution. NPCI's state and district tables, which assign each UPI payment (person-to-person and merchant) to the state or district of the payer's registered address. About 39% of UPI value has no state; we use only the classified part, so all figures here understate true activity (all-India, including unclassified, would be about ₹20,900 per person per month). The address rule is as described by the data archive; we could not confirm it on npci.org.in.

Period. July–September 2026, compared with July–September 2025. The national unclassified share was the same in both (38.9% and 39.0%), but that does not guarantee each state's missing share was stable, so treat state growth rates with care. District data only start in March 2026, so city growth rates are not yet available.

Per resident. Classified UPI value in the quarter ÷ population ÷ 3. State population: MoHFW 2026 projection. City population: World Population Review's 2026 estimate (retrieved 2 November 2026). World Population Review does not say what area each figure covers, and for most cities it is a metro area wider than the district(s) NPCI reports: Kolkata's 15.4 million is about 3.5 times Kolkata district and Hyderabad's 11.9 million about 3 times Hyderabad district, so their per-resident figures are understated. Pune's 7.9 million is below Pune district's own 2011 Census count (9.4 million), so Pune is overstated. The data table also gives a Census-based estimate for the districts themselves (2011 population on today's boundary, grown at its 2001–11 pace relative to its state): on it Bengaluru is ₹71,800, Mumbai ₹67,300, Hyderabad ₹61,200 and Kolkata ₹24,900. Local unclassified shares are also not known.

City = core district(s). Mumbai = Mumbai + Mumbai Suburban; Bengaluru = Bengaluru Urban; Chennai = Chennai district as enlarged in 2018 (assumed to be NPCI's boundary); Hyderabad, Pune, Kolkata, Ahmedabad = their own district; Delhi = all of Delhi.

11 · Limits

What this data can't tell us

Spending per person. UPI adds users and merchants every month, so UPI per resident mixes adoption with spending.

Category by place. NPCI doesn't publish categories by state or district.

Most of the growth. NPCI's "Others" is 45% of merchant value and took 58% of last year's increase (66% including categories moving on or off NPCI's list).

Cause and effect. Tax changes, rate moves and festival timing overlap, with no untouched comparison group. The verdicts are associations backed by several sources, not causal estimates.

Revisit after the festive season. This edition runs to September 2026. Diwali fell on 20 October in 2025 but falls on 8 November in 2026, and Navratri and the big online sales moved from late September to October, so October on its own will mislead. We will revisit clothing, electronics, online marketplaces and department stores once October–November 2026 data are out (expected December 2026), comparing September–November with the same months a year earlier.

12 · Method

Formulas, raw data and checks

Formulas

Growthvalue(this period) ÷ value(same period a year earlier) − 1, on months published in both periods. Average paymentvalue ÷ number of payments CAGR(value last year ÷ value base year)^(1 ÷ years) − 1, matched months. Share of merchant UPIcategory value ÷ all merchant value, same months. Contributionvalue this year − value last year; residual = total increase − named categories. Consumption part of UPI growthln(1 + PFCE growth) ÷ ln(1 + merchant UPI growth). Logs, because growth compounds: (1 + UPI growth) = (1 + consumption growth) × (1 + share gain), and we use an exact, order-independent log-accounting split. FY26: ln(1.085) ÷ ln(1.265) = 0.082 ÷ 0.235 = 35%; dividing 8.5% by 26.5% (32%) would leave 1.4 points of compounding unassigned. FY24: 19% (a simple ratio would give 15%). Consumption = MoSPI private final consumption (PFCE) at current prices. Merchant UPI also contains about ₹15 lakh crore a year of investment and repayment flows, so the share-gain part is not all cash switching. Cash share1 − digital consumer payments ÷ PFCE; digital = UPI merchant + Aadhaar Pay + FASTag + card and wallet payments at shops (+ card and wallet "other" payments for the high digital estimate), minus UPI to brokers, lenders, card bills and digital gold. Break-adjusted growth (sensitivity)last 12 months ÷ (pre-break months × (Sep 2025 ÷ Aug 2025) + post-break months of the earlier year) − 1 Per residentclassified UPI value ÷ population ÷ months. City population 2026: World Population Review's estimate; a Census-trend estimate for the districts (2011 population on today's boundary × (1 + r × state growth 2011–26), r = district ÷ state growth in 2001–11) is in the city data table. State share change100 × ln(share this FY ÷ share last FY), share of classified UPI value.

Data notes and checks

Checks. All 1,157 plotted values and every number in the text were checked against the raw files, primary sources or earlier checks. Values changed after review, and all 218 scoreboard fields, were then recomputed from raw data. The data, formulas and code behind each figure are at github.com/sdchak89/india-consumption-tracker.

Cash model. Extension of Bhuyan, RBI Bulletin (Oct 2024; the article does not represent RBI's views). Our components reproduce the article's FY23 and FY24 digital totals exactly; PFCE is on MoSPI's new 2022-23 base (the article used 2011-12).

Debt collection. NPCI did not announce a reclassification, and it cannot be verified from published data. Our reading rests on the size and timing of the September 2025 drop and the matching average payment of the credit-card bill category published in November 2025; confirming it needs an NPCI mapping notice.

Prices. UPI figures are nominal. In a Jan–Aug 2026 test using approximate category CPI proxies (inflation 0.4–10.9%), growth fell by 0.5–14.1 points and none of the 26 tested series changed sign.
